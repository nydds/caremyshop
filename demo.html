    Why the guard rails matter

    Anyone can generate a nice-sounding reply. Ours can be posted.

    A public reply is a legal document set in a friendly font. These four rules run on every draft before you ever see it — turn one off above and watch the draft get risky.

    01

    No refunds or prices in public

    A public “we will refund you” invites every other customer to ask for one, and turns a complaint into a negotiation. Money is settled on the phone or at the counter; the reply only says we will make it right.

    02

    No treatment or client details

    Insurance, diagnoses, invoices, appointment history, who said what in your chair — none of it belongs under a review. This is what keeps a dental reply HIPAA-safe and a salon reply out of a screenshot war.

    03

    No excuses, no blame

    Staff shortages, “that is our policy”, the customer’s own behaviour: all of it reads as defence in public. A reply may explain what changed. It never explains why it was not really our fault.

    04

    Signed by a person

    Every reply ends with the owner’s name and the shop. Anonymous “management” replies are what customers get from chains — a signature is what they get from a shop that stands behind its answer.
